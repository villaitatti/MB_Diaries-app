Just as we were starting to pay some calls in Cambridge, Mrs. Gardner telephoned for us, and then came and took us to Fenway Court. In beauty and taste it far surpassed our expectations, which were high. There is very little to find fault with, and endless things to praise! The pink of the walls of the inside court is rather a mistake, it looks dingy and dilapidated a bit, and the glass roof is badly treated. Also I dislike the Bardini blue25 of the stair-case and its corridor, but that is about all. The rest is too lovely to attempt to describe. I though there was remarkably little there that was not of Bernhard’s choosing, but that little annoyed him intensely, and hurt him too. They are all purchases of the last few years, since she ceased to listen to what he said.

There is the Chigi “Tintoretto” (a Domenico) which he refused at £4,000 and she bought on her own account for £8,000. As he had spoken of this, she told him it was a “present”; she is really a monumental liar. But this lie was a compliment to him, really.
