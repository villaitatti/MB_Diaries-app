She has had years of training in Greek, but was taught Greek literature rather “as an illustration to Goodwin’s Grammar”, her professor. She has read very little, Scott, Shakspeare, [sic] Thackeray, no French, almost no German. She still has the youthful feeling of always wanting to be first, and desiring everyone to look up to her and think her clever. If she stayed in Europe a while, under wholesome conditions, she would get not that desire perhaps, but the expectation of realizing it knocked out of her!

I read Laugel’s Oreille et la Musique, and gave Bernhard a music lesson.

He began Rosny’s Bilatéral, lent me by Vernon Lee.

Loeser wants to make up old scores, and has invited us to lunch. Giving meals seems a sort of instinctive habit with him, a kind of automatic reaction which occurs when the thought of an acquaintance presents itself to his mind.

Friday, March 23, 1894, “Good” (!) Friday

Finished at the dentist’s!
