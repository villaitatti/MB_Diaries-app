Then we called for Alfred Burton, who took us out to his home at Newton. On the way he told us wonderful tales of his adventures on a North Pole expedition, and on the expedition to Java to observe a solar eclipse. He said a priest lived near where he and his party stationed themselves, who had made friends with the monkeys of the forest. He fed them every day, and they were friendly with him. Berton and his men went over to see him and said they would like to see the monkeys. So the priest went out into the open and chattered up into the sky. Presently a rustling was heard in the distant tree-tops, coming nearer and nearer, and after awhile 4 or 500 monkeys swung into view. Burton and men had bananas to feed them, but the monkeys all waited for the King before beginning the feast. After a time the King appeared, a grey old monkey about 4 ft. hight, wrinkled and wizened. He advanced quite alone to Burton, laid one hand on Burton’s arm to prevent any undue familiarity and took a banana He stepped back some paces and ate it, deliberately peeling it, and pulling off the little inside fibres, all the other monkeys watching him. In this way he took 4, eating them one at a time. Then he gave the signal and the other monkeys swooped in, watching, fighting, clamouring.
