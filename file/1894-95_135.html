We studied Mantegna in the morning, and read Vasari. Had a lecture on “What I don’t know about Verocchio” from B.B. in the afternoon, and after tea a little talk about aesthetic emotion. It began with Bernhard’s wail that he could not write about the Florentines, because he no longer enjoyed them, as a school. He looked back to a time when he “enjoyed” all of Pollaiuolo, even such a thing as the Berlin Annunciation. Now he recognized that it gave him pleasure (though he did not analyze it at the time) as a piece of “instantaneously effective archaeology”.

Why do we get more pleasure from art than from nature? (if we do) It is a peculiar enhancement of life to know that man did it, not a god. This is an aesthetic emotion in so far as it is a real leap of life upwards, giving you a greater sense of power.

From a steamboat a great machine you get a certain sense of power, but of a less intense quality. Machinery only increases the commodities of life, and adds to the life itself.
